## Why this tool? and the causal chain of a failure

Click a tool call (or press Enter on its button in the graph list, or on the focused tool card): its popup has a **Why this tool?** disclosure (a native `<details>`: Tab to it, Enter or Space opens it). Every line is backed by an observed event; what cannot be proven is said so.

- **Reasoning observed just before this call**: the thinking that sits right before the call, in the same agent's own conversation, matched by the call's `tool_use_id`. Parallel calls of one batch share the reasoning that precedes the batch. Anything separated from the call by a result, an answer or a prompt is not attributed to it.
- **No reasoning observed just before this call**: the call is in the history and nothing precedes it. This is a finding, not an error.
- **Reasoning not disclosed by the runtime**: the runtime redacted the thinking; no text is invented.
- **Not provable: ...**: the call has no `tool_use_id`, is no longer in the kept history (conversations are bounded), or older messages were dropped. "Unknown" is never shown as "none".

A failed call also gets a **Causal chain of this failure**, an ordered list from the failure back to the prompt: the error text, the reasoning before the call, then for a sub-agent the dispatch that started it (who dispatched whom, with the full prompt and the parent's reasoning before the dispatch), and finally the **latest prompt observed before it** in the root agent's conversation. A hop needs the dispatch message with the same `tool_use_id` and a *verified* parent-child link; otherwise the chain stops with `Chain stops: ...` and says why. The prompt is an ordering fact (the latest one seen before), not a claim that it caused the failure. The derivation lives in `web/lib/tool-reasoning.ts`.

## What the action cards show

On the canvas, a tool card carries a small shape on its left that says what kind of action it is, so you do not have to read the label: a page (Read), a page with a plus (Write), a magnifier (Grep, Glob), a pencil (Edit, MultiEdit, apply_patch), a notebook (NotebookEdit), a checklist (TodoWrite), a globe (WebSearch, WebFetch), a speech bubble with a question mark (AskUserQuestion), a fork (Task, Agent sub-agent), and for Bash a prompt chevron and cursor, or, when the command line runs one of them (the first one wins): a node on a line (`git`), a rounded square with a node (`gh`), a snake (`python`), a flask (a test runner: `pytest`, `pnpm test`, `node --test`, `cargo test`), stacked containers (`docker`) or a parcel (a package manager: `pnpm install`, `pip install`). The shape comes from the tool name and, for Bash, from the command line only. MCP tools keep the generic card. While the call runs, the magnifier sweeps, the cursor blinks, the git node breathes, the globe turns, the bubble swells while an answer is awaited, a dot travels down the fork, the snake crawls and bubbles rise in the flask; with reduced motion (OS setting or Freeze motion) the glyphs are still. A shape says which command was issued, never that it succeeded.

A tool card or a discovery card is drawn only with its agent: when **Hide inactive** or a closed branch hides the agent, its cards leave the canvas too (a selected card keeps its agent shown), and **Fit to view** does not frame them. Tool cards are drawn above agent labels and message bubbles, so those texts are kept off the cards: a label moves aside, shortens to one line or is hidden, and bubbles fold into their count chip or are hidden, rather than sitting unreadable under a card. A halo label too long for its chip is cut with "…" and keeps its member count, e.g. "Session payments-api · ref… (4)". A label counts as drawn: its token line under the context bar ("4.3k estimated / 1M tokens") and its LEAD / MAIN pill are kept off the cards too, and a label hidden for a card takes its token line with it. Links and parent lines pass under agent labels: each line of a label sits on a small rounded plate of the scene background, so a line crossing the label is hidden only where the text is and drawn up to it (from the node's rim to the first line of text, and again past the label), never through the letters. The plate stays opaque while an agent appears or fades out. The token line under the context bar has a plate as wide as its text ("190k / 200k tokens (incomplete)" included), never narrower than the bar, so no end of it floats over a link or a halo. The plan and the drawing wrap a label name by the same measure (the canvas's, with the node's own radius), so the context bar keeps its place when the plan hides or shows the label.

**Fit to view** (and the automatic framing) frames the agents with their labels at the size they are drawn (enlarged below 100 %), their tool and discovery cards, the halos and the workspace frames, between the top bar and the row of canvas controls (Legend, Tour, zoom, View) that sits on the playback bar. While the scene grows, an agent, a card or a frame that appears outside the frame is brought in within a few frames (the camera catches up, then eases). **Fit to view** takes the same catch-up: zoomed in far, the camera comes back to the framing faster at first, then eases. With reduced motion, the camera jumps to the new framing in both cases. Only floating UI that is not a bar (the Conversation pill, a toast, an open legend or View panel) can still cover part of the canvas.

**Zoomed out, text is hidden rather than shrunk.** Agent names and states, tool-card text, message-bubble text and the secondary canvas text (stats, cost pills, the context percentage, token counts, discovery text, particle labels, the model caption, phase titles) are not drawn smaller than about 10 px on screen; the secondary text keeps 11 px wherever it is drawn. Link bubbles still follow the zoom until their level hides them. The session, team and workflow titles above the halos keep their size at every zoom and move off the workspace headers and the collapsed Conversation pill. Below 100 % an agent's name and state keep that size instead of shrinking; where they would overlap, the labels of less important agents shorten to one line ("name · state") or are hidden first (the selected, hovered or focused agent, then a lead or main agent, then an agent in error, then active agents keep theirs). Under 35 % no agent label is drawn. Zoomed out under 100 % (and back above it once you pass 100 %), a tool card becomes its icon: the action shape, a ring for MCP, a dot for other tools without a shape, in the same colours, with the same dashes for an unresolved call, a cross in the corner for a failed call and the same spinning ring while it runs (with reduced motion, a still half ring instead). An agent's message bubbles become their count chip ("…2"), placed right of the agent's label. Under 100 % too, the context percentage, discovery text, particle labels and phase titles are hidden, and the model caption inside a node only appears zoomed in far enough for it to reach 11 px; token counts under an agent's label, and the stats boxes and cost pills you turn on, keep 11 px and are hidden under 60 %. A level that appeared is kept until the zoom falls 8 % under its threshold, so text does not blink while you zoom around it. Nothing is lost: names, states and tool calls with their outcome stay in the graph list read by screen readers, the tooltip and the popups give the details, and the Conversation panel keeps the messages.

**Finding a hidden agent name** ([#594](https://github.com/j0hn-42/agent-lens/issues/594)). When the label placement hides at least one agent name, a button in the row of the canvas controls, before the zoom buttons, says how many: "8 names hidden" (*8 noms masqués*; "1 name hidden", *1 nom masqué*). The number comes from the placement of the frame drawn: it counts the agents whose node centre is in the canvas area left free by the bars and the open side panels, and whose name is not drawn. That area is measured at most once a second, so after a panel opens or closes the count can lag by about a second; a node under the collapsed Conversation pill or under the button itself is still counted. A label shortened to one line ("name · state") still shows the name, so it is not counted, and neither are agents off screen, hidden by **Hide inactive** or inside a folded branch. Under 35 % it counts every agent drawn, since no name is drawn there. With no name hidden there is no button (it never reads 0). The button appears, goes or changes its number only once the count has held for half a second with the camera at rest, so a zoom or a fit does not make it blink. While the count is changing it keeps its place and shows no number, only the name of the list it opens (**Sessions and agents**): a number on the button is always the count of the frame drawn. Sitting before the zoom buttons, its coming, going or width never moves **Zoom**, **Fit to view** or **View**. Zooming in does not always bring the names back: from 100 % the labels grow with the zoom, so labels that overlapped still overlap (with the team of 10 of the demo at 1920 x 1080, twelve presses of Zoom in still left 3 to 6 names hidden, the count changing as agents leave the window). It opens the **Sessions and agents** list, like L, and moves the focus to the list (the row that takes Tab) when the list is already open. It stays while it has the keyboard focus, without a number once no name is hidden, so the focus is not lost. Its accessible name is its visible text, its tooltip says why names are hidden and names the list (with its L key unless single-key shortcuts are off), and it is not announced when the number changes, since the number follows the zoom (screen readers have every name in the graph list anyway). Under 420 px wide it goes on the line above the zoom buttons rather than past the window. Whether the controls fold into the **Canvas controls** button of the playback bar does not depend on the count: room for the button is kept whenever it can be shown. Folded, it comes last, under the zoom buttons (the block scrolls; Tab brings it into view). The name of a hidden agent can also be reached:
- by hovering its node or giving it the keyboard focus (arrows on the graph): its label is placed first and the tooltip gives its name, state, model and task; a selected agent keeps its label;
- in the command palette (Ctrl+K), group *Agents*, which selects it;
- in the **Sessions and agents** list (L), under its session;
- in the graph list read by screen readers, which names every agent.

Some tool calls have their own card content in the popup and the transcript. Every figure comes from the call's own input; when the input does not carry it, nothing is shown.

- **Edit / MultiEdit**: a sentence such as "3 lines replaced by 2" (lines of `old_string` and `new_string`, summed over the blocks for MultiEdit, with the block count). It counts the two strings, it is not a diff, and it describes the request, not proof that the edit was applied. The verb ("replaced by", "inserted", "text removed from N lines", since `old_string` may be part of a line) is used only when every block is of one kind; a MultiEdit that mixes them says "old: 3 lines, new: 2 lines". A call that ended in an error, was cancelled, or has no result after the expiry delay says so instead: "call failed, not applied", "call cancelled, not applied" or "no result received, not known to be applied", followed by "asked old N lines, new M lines", and the lines below are read "Asked old:" / "Asked new:" (never "Removed:" / "Added:"). In the Conversation panel the card knows an error or a cancellation (the result was observed); it does not know an expiry, which has no result row, so it keeps the neutral request wording. A MultiEdit whose blocks are incomplete, or a call whose strings are all empty, shows no summary. `apply_patch` keeps `+N -M`, counted from the patch text.
- **Write**: the number of lines of the content written. It does not say whether the file already existed, because the call does not prove it.
- **Read**: the line range requested (`lines 10–59`, or `from line 100` when only an offset was given), from the call's `offset` and `limit`. Nothing is shown when the call states neither.
- **Other runtimes**: tools named differently are drawn like the one they correspond to: Codex `shell` and `exec_command` (Bash), `update_plan` (TodoWrite), `web_search_call` (WebSearch); Copilot `bash`, `powershell`, `view` (Read), `edit` (Edit), `create` (Write), `grep` (Grep), `glob` (Glob), `web_fetch` (WebFetch), `task` (Task). The tool name shown is still the one the runtime reported.
- **apply_patch** (Codex): the patched files with their operation (add, update, delete), `+N -M` counted from the patch text, and the patch lines. The event keeps only the first 500 characters of a patch: when it was cut, the files seen are listed, the card says so, and no count is shown.
- **TodoWrite**: "N of M done" and "N in progress", counted from the list the call carries (a Codex plan counts the same way).
- **WebFetch**: the host of the URL on its own line.
- **Task / Agent**: the sub-agent type and the short description.
- **NotebookEdit**: the notebook, the cell and the edit mode, when the call states them.
- **Bash**: when the command runs `git` or `gh`, a pill names each subcommand as written (`git commit`, `gh pr create`), read from the command line itself. A second row lists Python, test, docker and package-manager calls (`python analyse.py`, `pnpm test`, `docker compose up`, `pnpm install`). A pill says the command was issued, not that it succeeded: the outcome is the tool's state. No exit code or duration is shown, because the events do not carry them.
- **Result pills** (tool popup and Conversation panel): once a call is seen to succeed, a pill shows what its own output proves. For a Bash command line that runs `gh`, a link **PR #N** or **issue #N** (opens in a new tab), when the output has exactly one line that is a plain `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/pull|issues/<n>` URL (`gh pr create` and `gh issue create` print it). A sub-path or an anchor (`/files`, `#issuecomment-…`), two different URLs, a failed call, or a URL on the last line of an output cut at the 200-character capture limit give no link. For Grep and Glob, **N files** when the output starts with the tool's own `Found N files` header; lines are never counted, because a cut output proves nothing.
- **File cards from a search** (canvas discoveries, #438): a Grep in `files_with_matches` mode (or with no mode set) that is seen to succeed and whose output starts with its own `Found N files` header gets one **File** card per path it lists, next to its tool card, at most 5 cards per call (`SEARCH_DISCOVERY.maxPerCall` in `web/lib/canvas-constants.ts`). Grep prints paths relative to the working directory, and each whole path line under the header counts: reading stops at the first empty line, line with a leading or trailing space, or `path:12:` line, and the last line of an output cut at the 200-character capture limit is dropped. A Glob's output is a bare list without the header, so its text proves nothing; its cards come from the structured result Claude Code records in the transcript (`filenames`, `numFiles`, `truncated`), forwarded by the extension only when `truncated` is false, every name is a distinct single-line string and `numFiles` equals the list length (at most 20 names forwarded, 5 drawn; the count shown is `numFiles`). A truncated or inconsistent result, or a source without that result (the hooks), gets no Glob card. The graph list, the tooltip and the popup say where a card comes from ("File listed by Grep, 1 of 47 files the call reported; 3 shown, 44 not drawn"); the paths not drawn are in no card, and the count is the header's for a Grep and `numFiles` for a Glob. A failed or cancelled call, a Grep in content or count mode, an output without the header, a path listed twice or a call without a `tool_use_id` (its cards would have no stable id) gets no card; a replayed result adds none.
- **Flow to the file cards** (canvas, #384): a thin line with an arrowhead runs from a Grep or Glob tool card to each file card it produced, and one small chip travels along each line for 3 seconds of session time after the call completed (`SEARCH_FLOW.chipWindowS`); then the line and arrowhead stay, static, as long as the card, including a card kept by Pin cards or the pause; a paused playback stops the chips at once. A line exists only when the card's `tool_use_id` and agent match a tool call that is on screen and complete; a card with no such call, or a call still running, failed, cancelled or expired, has none, and the line goes when the card fades. A call whose `tool_use_id` arrived only with its result has no line: its cards carry the id, the call does not, so no call matches them. At most 5 lines (and chips) per call. The direction, call to files, is carried by the arrowhead and by the text of the graph list ("File listed by Grep, ..."), never by the motion. With reduced motion, Freeze motion or the **links** layer calmed, the chips are gone and the lines and arrowheads stay. The lines carry no text.
