## Language

Settings > Appearance > **Language** (a native select, so it works with the keyboard and screen readers) offers *Automatic (browser language)*, *Français* and *English*. The choice applies at once, is kept in this browser only (local storage, never sent to the relay) and "Automatic" forgets it. Each language is written in its own language, and `lang` is set on the translated blocks so a screen reader pronounces them correctly.

The interface is translated step by step, so for now the language covers only:

- the canvas controls (the Zoom group, the View button and its panel: Freeze motion, the per-layer toggles, Pin cards, Hide unanswered calls after);
- the Settings dialog in full (appearance, sound, sessions, presentation, notifications, keyboard, preferences). The theme names Catppuccin, Midnight, Graphite, Neon, Ember and Anthropic are proper names and stay as they are; *High contrast* is translated.
- the quota gauges of the top bar (their text is in your language and carries its own `lang`).
- the [Graph truncated](relay-and-losses.md#graph-truncated) notice (its sentence, **Details** and the counters by reason, the dismiss button and its announcement), which carries its own `lang`.

- the whole top bar (#373, #500, #501): the bar declares the interface language with `lang`, and so do its translated passages. Buttons and tooltips: *Hide inactive* (*Masquer les inactifs*, worded as in Settings, its accessible name and tooltip too), Notifications, mute, full screen, Commands, Settings and Keyboard shortcuts, and the seven panel buttons (*Fichiers et répétitions*, *Conversation*, *Contexte*, *Système*, *Coût*, *Chronologie*, *Statistiques*; the Context tooltip in VS Code is translated too). The Sessions button ("Sessions : Toutes les sessions", its tooltip, "n sessions non observées", the hidden "nouvelle activité dans une autre session" and "graphe incomplet, pertes listées dans le panneau Sessions" read after the ⚠ of a loss), the *More* button (*Plus*) under 1024 px and its name with the active filters (the whole name is in your language), the *View controls* and *Side panels* groups, the *Scope* selector (its name, its tooltip, its options, the sentence that says what is scanned and the discovery ceilings, which keep their "at least" as *au moins*), the connection state (*EN DIRECT*, *CONNECTÉ*, *CONNEXION*, *HORS LIGNE*, *DÉMO*, with the reconnect detail and the *Retry now* and *Exit demo* buttons) and the totals read by screen readers (agent counts, tokens, cost, the part that belongs to no agent) are in your language too, with the same figures and the same qualification (*au moins*, *estimé*, *non communiqué*). The attention counter is spelled out instead of the former "2 waiting / 1 error": "2 agents waiting / 1 agent in error" (*2 agents en attente / 1 agent en erreur*), with an accessible name that starts with that text, then says what a click does, and a tooltip. What the relay or the link wrote in English (a runtime problem such as "OpenCode: database not found.", a failed change of scope, a reconnect detail the interface does not know) is shown as it came and declares `lang="en"`. The playback bar words the connection state in your language too (*EN DIRECT*, *HORS LIGNE*, see the playback bar below); the panel titles follow the language (see the panel titles below) and the words drawn by the canvas stay English. In French the bar keeps its two rows from 1024 px, no taller than in English (measured in the demo at 1440, 1280, 1100 and 1024 px, with the room left in row 1 logged by `tests-a11y/e2e/topbar-layout.e2e.ts`; with an attention counter, a scope selector and the **Hide inactive** toggle drawn at once, measured on the real top bar alone in a browser at 1024 px, the bar wraps to 4 rows (5 with the three discovery ceilings written out) in French and in English alike (#372: the one toggle took the place of two buttons and made that row 1 about 260 px narrower in English and 320 px in French, the height did not change), so French is no taller than English there, but the two-row layout does not hold with all three. At 320 px in French nothing leaves the window or scrolls sideways, and no text of the bar is cut by an ellipsis: the Sessions button wraps its content (the label on one line, the counts and the "not observed" suffix below it) instead of squeezing the label (#595; measured in French and in English at 320, 390 and 768 px with the label of the All view, by `tests-a11y/e2e/sessions-button-reflow.e2e.ts`). The label of a selected session is data and wraps the same way (it is allowed to break inside a long word); that case is not measured with a very long name, and the accessible name is the whole visible text.)

- the agent card in full (model, context window, freshness and silence, loop health, git strip, delegation report, issue links, the card of a node that left the graph) and the Sessions panel (title, search and filters, session actions and states, announcements). In the card and the panel every figure keeps its qualification ("at least" becomes "au moins", "estimated" "estimé", "not reported" "non communiqué"). The columns of the panel (their names, and the accessible wording of every cell: *modèle non communiqué*, *branche non enregistrée*, *au moins 12k estimé jetons*, *durée inconnue*, *début de la session non enregistré*, *estimation du coût du contexte … incomplète*), the roles of the agent rows (*sous-agent*, *coéquipier*) and the announcement of agents that stop reporting (*… n’émet plus, dernier état connu affiché*) follow the language too, with the same figures and the same qualifiers; durations such as `3m 12s` and the `≥` and `~` marks are figures and stay as drawn. The canvas draws an English status line under each agent, and it stays English there and in the text tree of the canvas. In French an agent row quotes that line exactly as the canvas draws it, in an element declaring `lang="en"`, then glosses it in French, as the legend does. The canvas draws that line with one function, and the row quotes the same function (a test fails if the canvas draws anything else). So a teammate at work quotes its activity and glosses that activity (*working : Au travail*, *idle : Inactif* even while its state still says thinking), and a stale agent quotes its last known state (*last known state: Waiting for permission : en attente d’autorisation depuis 7 min*), never a live word. A fresh agent quotes its state word (*thinking : Réflexion*). A row that shows a tool name shows the tool as it came and quotes no word. The canvas may shorten a long line to fit under the node; the row quotes it in full. The quote and its gloss are never cut by an ellipsis: the text wraps, and on a stale row it takes its own line under the agent name, down to 320 px. The Σ totals (*Σ ~$0.129 · 13k estimé (incomplet)*, *inconnu* when no agent reported a token count, their accessible names *total de la branche*, *total de la session*, *total de la famille* and the explanation of the incomplete badge), the relative time of the Activity column (*il y a 2 min*, *à l’instant*) and the agent counts of the panel header (*30 agents : 0 actif · 4 derniers connus · 26 terminés*) are in your language too, with the same figures and qualifiers; no passage of the panel is marked English any more except the quoted state word. Names, paths, branches, model names, git commands and what an agent returned are data and stay as they came.

- the command palette (its title, search field, group names, commands and their descriptions, details such as "current view" or "finished", the result count and the key hint) and the legend (its toggle, the "n symbols on screen" line, every entry and the footer line). The panels are named *Fichiers et répétitions*, *Conversation*, *Contexte*, *Système*, *Coût*, *Chronologie* and *Statistiques*. The titles of the panels that have one follow the same names (see the next item). A command is found by its French name or by its English name ("full screen" finds *Basculer en plein écran*), and accents are optional when typing. The legend lists exactly the same entries as before, only the language of the text changes; the words the canvas itself draws (*Team*, *Workflow*, *LEAD*, *MAIN*) are quoted as drawn, and the state words the canvas draws under an agent stay English there. In French the state entries of the legend therefore quote the word the canvas draws (`done`, `waiting`, `tool call`) in an element declaring `lang="en"`, then give its French gloss (*done : terminé*), so that the word read on the canvas can be matched with an entry (WCAG 1.4.1, 1.3.3). The key cap of the zoom-to-fit shortcut reads *Maj+F* in French. Names of sessions and agents, branches and projects are data and stay as they came.

- the guided tour (#557): the legend button (*Visite guidée* in the demo, *Visiter cette session* in a real session) and its command in the palette, the offer made on the first launch (*Nouveau sur Agent Lens ?*, *Commencer la visite*, *Pas maintenant*), the card (*Étape n sur N*, *Précédent*, *Suivant*, *Aller à l’étape*, *Quitter la visite*, the notice that a step is no longer on screen) and every step, of the demo and of a real session. The steps say what they said in English: same order, same symbols, same figures (the loop badge still counts the same call 3 times in a row), and the same cautions (a state that only appears when the agent really asked, a symbol the demo cannot draw). The words the canvas draws (*LEAD*, *MAIN*) and the names of tools (*Grep*, *Glob*) are quoted as they are; the label of the highlighted agent, taken from its prompt, is data. A real-session tour keeps the symbols it was built from, not its words: changing the language while it runs rewrites it in the new language. The ring drawn around the explained element has no text.
- the Timeline panel (#373): its region (*Chronologie d’exécution*), the *Sequence* and *Table view* buttons (*Séquence*, *Vue en tableau*, whose name is their visible text), the scroll area, the accessible name of the canvas (*Chronologie de 3 agents, de 0:00 à 1:05*, with the plural of the language: *1 agent*, *0 agent*, *2 agents*), the columns and the states of the table (*Agent*, *État*, *Début*, *Fin*, *en cours* for a block still running; *Inactif*, *Réflexion*, *Appel d’outil*, *En attente d’autorisation*, *Erreur*, *Terminé*, the words of the legend of the panel), the table of messages between agents (*Heure*, *Type*, *De*, *Vers*, *Contenu*; *Envoi*, *Retour*, *Retour (erreur)*, *Message*), the message buttons (*orchestrator -> explore - ENVOI*), the line *+n messages antérieurs, voir la vue en tableau* (singular and plural by the rule of the language, the number written for it) and the empty state (*Aucune donnée de chronologie pour l’instant*), in the table and on the canvas. The words the canvas draws inside a block follow the language too, since the panel draws them itself: the fixed labels (*Réflexion…*, *Démarrage*, *Terminé*, *Autorisation*, *Repris*, *ÉCHEC*, *ANNULÉ*, and the suffix of a call that expired) and, in a block too narrow for its label, the letter of its state, one letter per state and distinct in each language (*I R O A E T* in French, *I T C P E D* in English). Times (`1:05`) are figures and stay as drawn. The name of the agents, the name of a tool and its arguments (`Read: src/foo.ts`) and the content of a message are data: they stay as they came, in an element declaring `lang="en"`, and so does the part of a block label that is a tool call, while its outcome word (*ÉCHEC*) is French. The pair filter chip under the timeline speaks the language too (it used to stay English there). The hidden table that reads the canvas no longer sticks out of the panel, and a long tool argument in the table view wraps instead of widening it. Measured in a browser against the demo in French, at 320 x 568 and 640 x 512, the header, the legend and the table view fit with nothing cut, covered or scrolling sideways (`tests-a11y/e2e/i18n-fr-panels-reflow.e2e.ts`); the demo has no link channel when it opens, so the *Séquence* button and the list of messages are not measured in a browser, only in jsdom.
- the titles of the panels (#557, WCAG 3.2.4): a panel has one name in a language, the same in the command palette, in the name of its top bar button and in its accessible name (the System panel also shows it as its title, the Files & repeats panel in capitals; the other headings contain it): *Fichiers et répétitions*, *Conversation*, *Contexte*, *Système*, *Coût*, *Chronologie*, *Statistiques* (*Files & repeats*, *Conversation*, *Context*, *System*, *Cost*, *Timeline*, *Stats* in English). The Cost overlay and the Stats overlay are drawn on the canvas and have no title of their own, so they keep their button and their palette name. The accessible name of a panel opened as a drawer under 768 px is that name, carried by a hidden element that declares the language (an `aria-label` cannot), so a screen reader pronounces it with the right voice. The headings Files & repeats, Timeline and Context show *FICHIERS ET RÉPÉTITIONS* (*FILES & REPEATS*, #518), *CHRONOLOGIE D’EXÉCUTION* and *CONTEXTE DU PROJET*. The French text of the Settings reset help names the panels the same way. The content of the Conversation, Files & repeats, Context and Timeline panels is translated (see the list of surfaces below); the Cost and Stats overlays are drawn by the canvas, in English, except the *Cost totals* text region, which is read in the interface language (see the Cost panel above).
- the context menu of the canvas and of an agent (#586): *Afficher les détails*, *Ajuster le zoom*, *Basculer les statistiques*, *Basculer la grille*, *Effacer l’historique* (then *Confirmer l’effacement de l’historique*), *Recharger les événements de la session*. The two-step confirmation and the check marks are unchanged.
- the playback bar and the review mode (#587): the badge (*EN DIRECT*, *CONNECTÉ*, *CONNEXION*, *HORS LIGNE*, *DÉMO*, the same words as the top bar), *Revue*, the play, speed and slider names (*Position de lecture*, "1:15 sur 1:02:05"), *Reprendre en direct*, *Effacer l’historique* and its confirmation, and the mark of a truncated history with the number of events dropped.
- the keyboard shortcuts dialog (#588): its title, the groups, the description of every shortcut (panels named as in the palette) and the single-key switch. The keys stay key names; only their words change (*Espace*, *Échap*, *Maj+F*).

- the content of the Conversation, Context and Files & repeats panels (#599, suite de #373): in **Conversation**, the pill and its accessible name, the header (*Appels d’outils*, *Exporter la conversation*, search), the agent tabs (*Tous*, *terminé*, unread), the pair filter (*Filtrer une paire*, its chip, its announcement and its empty state), the team chips, the role of each row (*RÉFLEXION*, *UTILISATEUR*, *ENVOI*, *RETOUR*, *RETOUR (ERREUR)*, *COMPACTÉ*), *Copier*, *Voir tout (+n caractères)*, the marker of dropped messages, the count of new messages, *Aller au dernier message*, the copy and export feedback and the words around each tool call (the edit summaries, *Bloc 1 sur 2*, *Avancement des tâches*, *lignes 5–14*, the result summary); in **Files & repeats**, the empty and incomplete states, the conflicts and who edited what, the tokens of each file and of the total, the steps of a file (*3 étapes observées (incomplet)*), *Lectures et appels répétés* and its summary (*Lectures et appels répétés : au moins 5 répétitions observées*, #518) and *Actualiser*; in **Context**, the project files and their states (*introuvable pour ce projet*, *Tronqué : les 64 premiers Ko sur 97 Ko*), the issues, *REPRISE* and *Aller au terminal*, *CAPACITÉS* (instructions, skills, MCP servers, with their *au moins* lower bounds) and the pull requests and issues of the session (*vérifications inconnues*, *il peut y en avoir d’autres*). Each panel declares its language with `lang`. What the data brings is shown as it came and declares `lang="en"`: file names and paths, the text of messages, thinking and tool calls, tool output, the content of CLAUDE.md and of the memory file, the resume command, branch names and the titles of pull requests and issues. A figure keeps its qualification: *au moins*, *estimé*, *incomplet*, *non communiqué*, and a count over a graph that lost events stays a lower bound.

To add a string, add its key to the file of its domain in `web/lib/messages/`, in **every** locale (fr and en side by side; `web/lib/messages.ts` only assembles the domains, #612); `scripts/messages.test.ts` fails on a missing key, a different `{placeholder}`, a French text that is still the English one or a key defined in two domain files. `web/tests-a11y/i18n-no-mixed-languages.test.tsx` renders Settings (each notification state, an invalid threshold included), the canvas controls, the Sessions panel (columns, agent rows in each state, the announcement of agents that stop reporting), the agent card, the command palette, the legend, the context menu (canvas and agent, confirmation included), the playback bar (live, offline, demo, review, confirmation, truncated history) the shortcuts dialog, the Conversation panel (pill, tabs, search, pair filter, every kind of message and of tool call, copy feedback), the Files & repeats panel (files, conflicts, steps, repeated reads and their summary, empty and incomplete states) the Context panel (files, issues, capabilities, resume, pull requests, every state) and the Timeline panel (every state of a block and every fixed label, the table view, Sequence, the messages and their cap, the pair filter, the empty and the incomplete states, the canvas drawing itself) in each locale and fails on any rendered string (visible text, accessible names, tooltips, descriptions) that is not a value of the current locale's catalog or of an explicit allowlist of proper names and symbols; a subtree that stays English is left out only if it carries the `data-i18n-exempt` attribute, and it declares `lang="en"` as well. The guided tour (card, offer, buttons, every step) and the titles of the panels are rendered by the same test, with no exemption and no allowlist of their own. When you translate a new surface, add it to that test. Still English for now: the sentences of the graph outline, of the canvas marks it mirrors and of the hidden-panels notice (only the name of the Files & repeats panel in them follows the language, so the name is the same everywhere, WCAG 3.2.4), the words drawn by the canvas for the Cost overlay (*Unattributed*, *BY TOOL*, the qualifiers *at least*, *estimated*, *incomplete* and the unit *ctx tokens* of the header) and for the Stats overlay (*3 tools · 1:05*), the Markdown file of a conversation export, the tool detail popup and the presentation subtitle (the caption of a transition, *orchestrator: Thinking → Complete*, and its name). Translated since #613: the accessible name of the Conversation pill (*Ouvrir le panneau Conversation. Dernier message utilisateur de main :* in French, then the preview of the message: the name now comes from two hidden elements, the sentence in the interface language and the preview declaring `lang="en"` like every message text, so a screen reader no longer reads an English message in a French sentence; the visible role, agent and preview are all in it), the text of the native notifications (browser and VS Code, see Notifications: same facts and qualifiers, composed by the page, shown as received by the extension), the presentation-mode badge (*Mode présentation · V pour arrêter*, named *Arrêter le mode présentation*) and its announcement (*Mode présentation activé* / *désactivé*, *Plein écran activé* / *désactivé* for full screen), the whole live region of the page that reads the connection, session and review mode aloud (the `ChromeAnnouncer`, which declares the interface language: *Connexion : en direct. Mode direct*, the connection state with the words of the top bar, the review instruction naming the button as the bar does, *Reprendre en direct*; a session name is data and is read as it is), the toast "No events to review yet" (*Aucun événement à revoir pour l’instant*, the same catalog sentence as the reason linked to the Review button; the toast "Review mode - press LIVE to resume" no longer exists: review mode is announced once, by that region) and the keyboard help the graph reads to screen readers (generated from the same table as the shortcuts dialog, in the language: *Flèches : Se déplacer entre les nœuds…*). The play button and its shortcut use the same words (*Lecture* / *Pause*, *Lecture / pause*). The playback-bar badge uses the words of the top bar (LIVE, OFFLINE, DEMO become EN DIRECT, HORS LIGNE, DÉMO) and pulses only while the relay is watching, in both languages. The Cost and Stats overlays are left in English on purpose (#373): their drawing functions (`drawCostSummaryPanel`, `drawStatsOverlay`) take no language, the Cost panel is a box of a fixed width whose header wraps from a measured width (`costHeaderWraps`), and the width of the Stats box is reserved by the overlay plan from the measured English text (`statsLabel`). A French text is longer (*au moins … estimé … jetons ctx*), so translating them would change measured widths that decide wrapping and label placement, and no measurement was made. The Cost figures keep their French equivalent in the *Totaux du coût* text region. To translate them later, the draw would have to receive the locale and the measures be redone in French at 320 px and at 200 % zoom.
To add a language, extend `LOCALES` and fill the table.
