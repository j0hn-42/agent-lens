## Narrow windows and phone width

- **Hidden panels**: below 900 px wide (or when the panels cannot all fit), panels are full-width sheets and only the newest open one is drawn. A line under the top bar then says "N panels hidden: <names>. Close the panel shown to see <name>.", where the names are in opening order and the last one is the panel that comes back when you close the one shown. The names are the open panels the layout does not draw, so the list is exact (Context is named Context, not Files, even though both share one slot); nothing is written when none is hidden. On a very short window the line is still announced to screen readers but the sheet takes the room, so it may be covered. When the [Graph truncated](relay-and-losses.md#graph-truncated) notice shows, it sits right under this line, and the sheet starts under both when there is room, even when it is the only open panel; the sheet is then shortened (it scrolls) rather than covering the notice. On a short window (or a narrow one where the top bar wraps), or when **Details** makes the notice taller than the room left, the sheet keeps only this line free and may cover the truncation notice, or the notice may cover the top of the sheet.
- **Top bar below 1024 px wide**: the bar stays one line (see [Top bar layout](top-bar.md#top-bar)); the panel buttons join the scope, the view filters, the quota gauges and the tools behind the **More** button ("More: scope, filters, quota and tools", it says whether it is expanded), an overlay that never adds a row; Escape or a click outside closes it. From 768 to 1023 px the Commands button stays in the bar, the panels stay docked and the controls keep their size (the drawers and the 44 px targets start under 768 px). Opening a panel from More closes it, and closing that panel gives the keyboard focus back to More. Screen readers still read the counts and totals in the bar; when **Hide inactive** is on More says so: "More · 1 filter", its name naming it, with the number of finished sessions it hides in All sessions ("More · 1 filter on: scope, filters, quota and tools (Hide inactive · finished sessions hidden: 3)"). Crossing 1280, 1024 or 768 px keeps the keyboard focus on the same control, More opening when the control moved into it. The Sessions button, the connection state and the attention counter stay in the bar; the Sessions button draws the selection, which ends in an ellipsis when the line is full (at least 10 characters stay drawn, at 320 px too), and under 1440 px the attention counter is a number, their names and tooltips giving the whole text. In the Timeline, the time labels are spaced for the panel width: fewer labels on a narrow panel, never two touching, and the last one is moved inside the edge rather than cut, or left out if it would then touch the one before.
- **Phone width (below 768 px)**: an open panel is a drawer the full width of the window, in the place of the sheets above (between the top bar and the playback bar, under the hidden-panels line and the truncation notice, which stay visible). It is a dialog: the keyboard focus moves into it and Tab cycles through it and, when they are drawn above it, the permission cards and the truncation notice (nothing else of the page); Escape or its Close button closes it and the focus goes back to the button that opened it (or to More when that button is folded). From inside a drawer the single-key shortcuts do not run. The controls of the top bar, the zoom block, the playback bar and the drawers' Close buttons are at least 44 x 44 px. On the canvas, the click and focus zone of a link-message bubble (and of its count chip) is at least 44 x 44 px too, the drawing staying smaller inside it; the zones of two bubbles never overlap, and a bubble hidden to avoid a collision is neither clickable nor reachable by Tab. Dragging and pinching the canvas have button equivalents: zoom in, zoom out and fit (which recentres the graph). Nothing forces an orientation. The connection state is the one the event stream proves (connected, reconnecting, offline); the view never says whether you are on the relay's machine or remote, because nothing in the stream proves it.
